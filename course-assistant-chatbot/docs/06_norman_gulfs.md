# Don Norman: Gulfs of Execution and Evaluation, Affordances

Don Norman's "The Design of Everyday Things" gives HCI some of its core
vocabulary.

The gulf of execution is the gap between what a user wants to do and the
actions the system makes available. If you want to save a file but cannot
figure out how, you are stuck in the gulf of execution. The gulf of
evaluation is the gap on the way back: after acting, can the user tell what
happened and whether it worked? An interface with poor feedback has a wide
gulf of evaluation.

Good design narrows both gulfs. Clear labels, visible actions, and natural
mappings narrow execution; immediate, understandable feedback narrows
evaluation. In a chatbot, the gulf of execution is naturally narrow (you
just type your question), but the gulf of evaluation can be wide: how do you
know whether the answer is trustworthy? Our chatbot shows its source
passages precisely to narrow that gulf.

An affordance is a property of an object that suggests how it can be used: a
button affords pressing, a slider affords dragging. Norman later emphasized
the term "signifier" for the perceivable signal of an affordance -- the
visual design that communicates "you can click this." Flat design's problems
often come from removing signifiers while keeping affordances.

Norman also describes the seven stages of action: forming a goal, forming an
intention, specifying an action, executing it, perceiving the system state,
interpreting it, and evaluating the outcome. The first four sit on the
execution side, the last three on the evaluation side.
