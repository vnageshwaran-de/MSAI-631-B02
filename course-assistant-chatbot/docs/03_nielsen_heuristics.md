# Nielsen's Ten Usability Heuristics

Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics (1994) are the most widely used
rules of thumb for judging interface usability:

1. Visibility of system status: keep users informed about what is happening
   through timely feedback, like progress bars and loading indicators.
2. Match between the system and the real world: speak the user's language and
   follow real-world conventions.
3. User control and freedom: provide undo and a clear emergency exit.
4. Consistency and standards: same words and actions should mean the same
   thing everywhere; follow platform conventions.
5. Error prevention: prevent problems before they occur, with confirmations
   and constraints.
6. Recognition rather than recall: make options visible instead of forcing
   users to remember information.
7. Flexibility and efficiency of use: accelerators like keyboard shortcuts
   let expert users speed up.
8. Aesthetic and minimalist design: avoid irrelevant information.
9. Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors: error messages in
   plain language that suggest a solution.
10. Help and documentation: searchable, task-focused help when needed.

These heuristics are the basis of heuristic evaluation, where a small group
of evaluators inspects an interface against the list. In our chatbot, showing
a loading indicator and displaying source passages are direct applications of
heuristics 1 and 9.
